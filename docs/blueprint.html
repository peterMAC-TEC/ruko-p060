The insight

A digital arrest is a capture, and the money leaves through the bank.

Scammers combine authority, fear and isolation (“stay on camera, tell no one”) for hours or weeks. Then the victim breaks FDs, takes gold loans and sends RTGS transfers, often at the branch and in many instalments. One Mumbai couple made 26 branch visits in 40 days. Recovering the money afterwards barely works, so prevention is the only thing that counts.

Positioning
Ruko is the safety layer between the scam call and the money. It breaks the isolation, brings in the family, makes the bank pause, and proves it works.

Ruko means “stop”, which matches the Prime Minister’s digital-arrest advice: “Stop, Think, Take action.” Banks pay, and families use it free through their bank. The timing suits banks:

  • From 1 Jan 2027, frauds done “under coercion or duress” count as fraudulent, which moves cost onto banks.
  • RBI’s April 2026 proposals cover a 1-hour hold and trusted-person approval for customers aged 70+. They are still proposals.
  • The Supreme Court is pressing RBI for a debit-hold procedure.
What makes Ruko the best

Eight pillars

P1

“Is this call real?”

A calm voice (with a keypad option) checks the real scam phrases: parcel, Aadhaar misuse, TRAI, “stay on camera”, “RBI verification”. Then it answers with one fixed fact: no police, CBI, ED, RBI or court arrests anyone or takes money on a video call. Real bank calls come from 1600 numbers.

ModerateKnowing the scam: 9% lost money vs 34%. The Kochi rescue turned on that one sentence.
P2

Silent “Tell my family”

One tap, nothing the scammer can see. The family is told what to say: “Are you safe? I’m with you. Hang up; I’ll call back in 2 minutes.” If someone told you to tell no one, that is the scam.

Strong51% of people who had someone intervene avoided losing money (FINRA).
P3

Smart pause at the bank

It warns only on real risk. The safe choice is always the biggest button. It reads behaviour instead of the reason the customer gives: new payee, broken FDs, a gold loan followed by RTGS, a live call, large cash withdrawals. A 1-hour hold, trusted-person approval for customers 70+, and the hold follows the person across banks.

StrongRandomised trial: scam payments 22% → 4% (−81%), and −94% on repeat. UK refund rules plus delay powers: −21%.
P4

Branch and fraud-desk co-pilot

A teller screen with 3 questions (“Do you trust this payee?”), a quiet room and official proof. One-tap “Call 1930” and “E-lock account”. The desk calls likely victims proactively, and the trusted contact is told within a fixed time.

StrongHong Kong’s proactive calls drove ≈half of its 2025 loss drop. UK branch protocol: £258m prevented.
P5

Ruko Lock

Savings are locked against digital transfer. Unlocking needs family or a branch visit, plus a 12–24 hour cooling-off. It is switched on by default during onboarding.

ModerateSingapore locked S$6.6bn with no reported fraud on locked money. Hong Kong made it opt-in and got ~3,200 users.
P6

Practice that protects

Interactive drills under 5 minutes, each for a specific scam and channel, mixed with genuine calls. Boosters every 2–4 weeks. A fictional agency, the stop word RUKO, and a kind debrief.

StrongInteractive practice works and static tips don’t. Repeated contact: 22.4% vs 8.6% fewer repeat victims (US RCT).
P7

Proof (the moat)

It measures stop rate with Ruko versus without, false alarms, time to stop, and money lost before the save. Over time it builds shared scam-script intelligence that banks and telcos can’t see on their own.

GapNo competitor publishes any effectiveness data. Banks need evidence for RBI and their boards.
P8

Reach everyone (after 28 Oct)

A basic-phone version by missed call, phone menu or SMS. The family’s phone and the branch carry the protection for elders who aren’t online. English, Hindi, Kannada, then the rest.

ContextOnly 41% of Indian elders own a smartphone, and 66% find digital tools confusing (HelpAge).

How every screen behaves

Design rules from the evidence

  1. Name the scam, then state the “never” rule.
  2. Calm first: “You are safe. You have done nothing wrong.”
  3. One physical action per screen, starting with “End the call”.
  4. The safe button is always the biggest one.
  5. No shame, ever: “This happens to doctors, officers and engineers.”
  6. Show proof instead of arguing, and always show 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in.
  7. Warn only on risk. Aim for warnings on fewer than 10% of routine payments.
  8. Text at least 20px, tap targets about 12mm, 7:1 contrast on warnings, every alert read aloud.

Trust and compliance, built in:

  • A separate consent for each purpose, checked with teach-back.
  • Each family member consents for themselves.
  • The elder is always the user, and adults are never monitored.
  • Ruko keeps signals, never audio.
  • Drills use a fictional agency and an ethics-consent form.
  • Privacy notices in English, Hindi and Kannada.
  • Data stays in India.
  • RBI items are labelled “proposed”.

Business

Banks pay; families are protected.

  • Primary: “Vulnerable Customer Protection” for banks, at ₹10–25 per enrolled senior per month, plus a ₹3–8 lakh 90-day pilot. Start with small finance and co-operative banks; they move faster and have fewer vendors.
  • Secondary: drill-based stop-rate audits (₹2–5 lakh per 100 people), an insurer add-on, an elder-care partner such as Emoha, and a low-cost family plan as an entry point.
  • First conversations: AU Small Finance Bank, a Bengaluru co-operative bank with many pensioners, then Emoha and HDFC ERGO.
  • Risks: RBI may finalise the rules differently. Free public tools and Truecaller push prices down. Bank sales take 6–18 months.
