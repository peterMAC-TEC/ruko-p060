## 1. Why people fall for it

- **Strong emotion crowds out scrutiny** (Langenderfer & Shimp 2001) [PR].
- **Being aroused, by fear or excitement, makes people more likely to comply.** In an experiment (Kircanski 2018, 139 adults), high arousal raised willingness to buy a falsely advertised product at every age; older adults were more willing overall.
  - **Advice from the study: delay decisions until the person is calm** [PR].
- **Scam compliance** (Fischer, Lea & Evans 2013): people who complied relied heavily on **signs of official authority** [PR].
- **Isolation** (FINRA Foundation, BBB and Stanford 2019, n=1,408):
  - victims were more likely to have had **no one to discuss the offer with**;
  - people who lost money scored higher on loneliness [NGO].
- **Prior knowledge protects:** of those who had **heard of the specific scam before, 9% lost money, versus 34%** of those who hadn't [NGO].
- **Cognition** (Rush studies):
  - low scam awareness predicts Alzheimer's [PR];
  - in a live mock-scam test (2023, 644 people, average age ≈86), **16.4% "converted"**, i.e. handed over personal details. **People with no cognitive impairment converted too.**
- **Why educated professionals fall for digital arrest** (news only, no peer-reviewed Indian study):
  - scammers deliberately pick people with social standing, who fear damage to their reputation;
  - victims are told that telling family will make things worse;
  - "virtual confinement" can last days.
- **India:** 2024 had 123,672 cases and ₹1,935.5 cr lost. Karnataka had fewer cases in 2025 but still lost ₹144.6 cr.

## 2. What reduces victimisation

**Practising on fake scams ("prebunking")**
- **Interactive practice works; tips don't** (Robb & Wendel, 1,000+ US adults; summarised by Boston College's CRR):
  - under 5 minutes judging fake versus real emails, with feedback after each one, **significantly improved detection** and didn't reduce trust in real emails;
  - **static tips and the government's own materials did not work**;
  - **the effect fades over time and is specific to the channel practised.**
- **Forewarning** (Stanford, people who had previously been defrauded):
  - a warning about the *same* scam held for 2 weeks;
  - a warning about a *different* scam still worked at 4 weeks.
- **Games and videos fade without boosters.** Cambridge's Bad News game cut belief in fake news ≈21%, but the effect faded within about 2 months; boosters help (Maertens 2025, n=11,759).
- **Short videos can work cheaply:** YouTube prebunking ads raised recognition of manipulation by about 5% for ≈$0.05 per view. A 2026 Instagram study (N=375k) found a 19-second video added **21 percentage points**, still present at 5 months, among people who took the quiz.

**⭐ The most important study for Ruko: CeDEx payment-warning trial** (Akesson, Gathergood & Quispe-Torreblanca 2023; randomised; ≈9,000 UK adults; mock bank app; real money incentives)

| Warning shown | Paid the scammer | Change |
|---|---|---|
| No warning (control) | **22%** | — |
| **Risk-based warning + prominent buttons ("Cancel", "Save for later", "Call the bank")** | **4%** | **−81%**, rising to **−94% by the third time** people saw it |
| Behavioural warnings (loss aversion, social norms) | — | Only −18%, and **no longer significant by the third time** (warning fatigue) |

- With the button design, genuine payments completed only fell from 57% to 50%.
- The UK payments regulator (PSR, 2025) agrees: **warn only on high-risk payments and give clear buttons to act on.** Warnings shown on every payment stop working.

**Cooling-off and friction**
- RBI's **April 2026 proposals**:
  - a 1-hour lag on transfers above ₹10k (merchants, bills and whitelisted payees exempt);
  - a trusted person must approve payments of ≈₹50k+ for customers aged 70+;
  - **a 24-hour wait before the trusted person can be changed**, so a scammer can't pressure the victim into swapping it;
  - banks want the threshold raised to ₹25k; the final rules aren't confirmed.
- **Singapore Money Lock:** 78k accounts locked S$6.6bn; unlocking takes a 12-hour cooling-off.

**Bringing in someone else**
- **51% of victims who had someone intervene avoided losing money** (FINRA 2019).
- UK Banking Protocol: £55.5m prevented in 2022; £258m since 2016.
- **Few people set up a trusted contact** (about 38–42% of US investors, and fewer among people 65+). **Make the family link the default during onboarding.**

**Safe words**
- Useful against **voice-clone "family emergency"** scams (FTC guidance).
- For "officer" scams, the right tool is **a "never" rule**: "No real officer will ever…".

**Wording that gets a frightened person to hang up**
- The PM's Mann Ki Baat message (Oct 2024): **"Stop, Think, Take action."**
  - No agency investigates by phone or video call.
  - No agency threatens you or asks for money.
  - **Feeling scared is itself the warning sign.**
  - Report it on 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, and tell your family.
- **This maps directly onto the name "Ruko".**

## 3. Talking to someone mid-scam

**What UK branch staff do (Banking Protocol):**
- ask discreetly what the payment is for, **assuming the customer may have been coached**;
- ask directly whether someone claiming to be police or the bank contacted them;
- take the customer to a **quiet area**;
- call the police even if they are unsure.
- The Ombudsman criticises scripts that staff simply read out.

**What the Kochi manager did (₹4.5 lakh saved):**
1. Stayed calm and private, with no confrontation.
2. Asked an open question: **"Do you trust this recipient?"**
3. Moved the customer **away from the phone**.
4. Said plainly that **"digital arrest" doesn't exist**.
5. **Showed proof**: news reports and RBI videos.
6. Then had him block the number, call 1930, e-lock the account and file a complaint.

**Shame:** 53% of people see victims as blameworthy (AARP), and 47% of victims blame themselves (FINRA). Blame stops people reporting and splits families apart.

**A script for family members and Ruko:**
1. **"Are you safe? I'm here. You've done nothing wrong."**
2. **One action:** "End the video call."
3. **One fact:** "Police never arrest anyone on a video call."
4. **Proof**, plus a promise to call back on a known number.
5. **Afterwards:** help with 1930 and the bank, and never ask "how could you?"

## 4. Designing for Indian elders

- **Device reality** (HelpAge India):
  - 2025: 71% can use a basic phone, **only 41% own a smartphone**, 13% use the internet, **5% bank online**; **66% find digital tools confusing** and 51% fear making mistakes.
  - 2024: 59% have no access to any digital device (48% of men versus 33% of women; 26% of those over 80).
- **Implication:** the smartphone app reaches only a minority of elders. **The family member's phone, the bank branch and a basic-phone route have to carry a big share of the protection.**
- **Voice versus keypad:** for low-literacy users in rural Gujarat, **keypad input beat speech recognition** (Patel, CHI 2009). Older adults' success with phone menus depends on memory.
  - **Design rule: voice first, keypad always available, at most 3 options per prompt.**
- **Accessibility floors:**
  - tap targets: 48dp minimum (≈9 mm), **aim for ≈12 mm for older adults**;
  - contrast: **7:1 on warnings** (WCAG AAA).
- **Onboarding:** done with a family member. Use **"teach-back" consent**: the elder explains in their own words what Ruko does and doesn't record.
- **No dark patterns:** **the safe option (Cancel / Pause) is always the visually dominant button.** This is the reverse of usual app design, and it's what made the CeDEx button design work.

## 5. Measuring the 10-elder trial honestly

**Precedents:**
- Rush 2023: live impersonation calls to people who had consented.
- Stanford: a 4-level coding of how people responded.
- **Singapore's National Simulated Scams Exercise** (Mar–Aug 2026): opt-in, government-impersonation scenarios, and **participants don't know when the call will come.**
- USENIX 2019: the review board barred spoofing a real contact the person knows.

**Ethics:**
- deception needs approval, minimal risk, and an **immediate debrief**;
- plan for embarrassment and stress;
- follow India's ICMR 2017 guidelines on research with vulnerable people.

**Recommended design:**
1. **Consent:** written plus teach-back, e.g. "Over 4 weeks you'll get practice calls; say RUKO or hang up any time." Name a family contact.
2. **Calls per person:** one baseline scam call (no Ruko) → set up Ruko → one scam call with Ruko (scripts A and B alternated between people) → **1–2 harmless control calls** to measure false alarms.
3. **Main measure:** an **ordered "furthest step reached" scale**: hung up < stayed more than 5 min < opened the mock bank < entered an amount < pressed pay < overrode the cooling-off. Also report the simple "stopped before transfer".
   - Why: real-world conversion is only ≈16–22%, so a yes/no measure may show no difference with 10 people.
4. **Safeguards:** a distress check after every call (1–5 faces scale), an immediate kind debrief, stop rules, no real OTPs or money, no real officer names, and a family member informed.
5. **Confound:** the baseline call itself acts as a forewarning, so some of the improvement is practice. Say so openly.

**Statistics with n=10:**
- An exact McNemar test needs **at least 6 people who changed, all in Ruko's favour** (5 of 5 gives p = .0625).
- 95% confidence ranges:
  - 8 of 10 stopped → **44–97%**;
  - 0 of 10 false alarms → **0–31%**.
- **Call it a feasibility pilot**, register the outcome measures in advance, and show each person's path individually.

## 15 design rules for Ruko

| # | Rule | How to test it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Name the scam and the rule**: "Digital arrest does not exist. Police never arrest or question anyone on a video call." | 100% of warnings include a scam name plus a "never" rule | 9% vs 34% (FINRA); Mann Ki Baat; Kochi |
| 2 | **Warn only on risk**, never on every payment | Warnings on fewer than 10% of routine payments | CeDEx; PSR on warning fatigue |
| 3 | **Every warning has 2+ big exits, and the safe one is the main button** ("Cancel", "Pause 1 hour", "Call my family") | The safe button is the dominant one and first in focus order | CeDEx: −81% to −94% |
| 4 | **Cooling-off by default, no early release during a live call** | The hold timer can't be bypassed | RBI proposal; Singapore |
| 5 | **Calm first**: "You are safe. You have done nothing wrong." | Every alert opens with a reassurance | Kircanski 2018 |
| 6 | **One physical instruction per screen**, starting with "End the call" | The first screen is always "end the call" | Kochi; the isolation tactic |
| 7 | **Break isolation**: one tap silently alerts family; "they told you not to tell anyone" is called out as a scam sign | SOS is at most 1 tap from home, with no confirmation dialog | FINRA: isolation predicts loss; 51% saved when someone intervened |
| 8 | **No shame language**: "This happens to doctors, officers and engineers." | An automated text check finds 0 banned words | AARP; FINRA |
| 9 | **Proof, not argument, plus the helpline** | Every hold and alert screen shows an official statement plus 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in | Kochi |
| 10 | **Drills are interactive, specific to the scam and channel, under 5 minutes, and repeated every 2–4 weeks**, mixed with genuine examples | Feedback on each item; at least 30% genuine items; boosters scheduled | Robb & Wendel; Stanford; Maertens |
| 11 | **Code word plus a callback for "family" requests; a "never" rule for "officer" requests** | Each drill type triggers the right tool | FTC |
| 12 | **The family alert tells them what to *say*** ("Are you safe? I'm with you. Hang up; I'll call back in 2 minutes") | The family screen leads with the script and a call button | Banking Protocol; AARP |
| 13 | **Works on a basic phone**: "Is this call real?" and SOS via missed call, phone menu or SMS, keypad input, at most 3 options | The full SOS flow works on a basic phone | HelpAge 2025; Patel 2009 |
| 14 | **Accessibility floors**: text ≥20px, tap targets ≥48dp (aim for 12 mm), 7:1 contrast on warnings, every alert read aloud in English, Hindi and Kannada | Automated audit | WCAG 2.2; Material Design; studies of older adults |
| 15 | **Measure honestly**: teach-back consent, outcomes registered in advance, control calls, debrief, distress checks, exact confidence ranges | The protocol document includes all of these; the dashboard labels seeded vs real data | Rush; Stanford; Singapore; ethics review guidance |
