# Research 1: How digital-arrest scams work in India (as of 9 Oct 2026)

How to read this:
- Source tags: **[GOV]** government or primary · **[COURT]** court orders · **[NEWS]** reputable outlets · **[LOCAL/SEC]** regional, aggregator or analysis sites · **[BLOG/PR]** blogs, vendors, NGOs.
- Every figure below is unverified unless it carries a [GOV] or [COURT] tag.
- RBI items are **proposals**, not final rules.

## 1. The scam, stage by stage

**Stage 0: Targeting**
- Scammers often already know the victim's Aadhaar-linked details or location.
- Hyderabad police describe typical victims as **well-educated retirees aged 60–80 whose children live abroad** (siasat.com, police statement).

**Stage 1: First contact**
- Common openers:
  - a courier or parcel "containing drugs" (FedEx, DTDC, DHL)
  - **TRAI** saying "your SIM will be cut in 2 hours" (TRAI says it never makes such calls [GOV])
  - "your Aadhaar or ATM card was found in a raid", or money-laundering charges
  - terror or national-security accusations, e.g. "Army photos sent to Pakistan"
  - **"your son or daughter has been arrested"**, sometimes with a cloned voice
- The call is then handed to a fake "NCB", "cyber cell" or "CBI" officer.

**Stage 2: Fake officers on video**
- The call moves to WhatsApp or Skype video.
- Props: **uniforms, sets made to look like police stations, forged warrants with court or RBI seals**, and translators supplied for regional languages (Kochi).
- I4C states that **CBI, police, customs, ED and judges never arrest anyone over video calls** [NEWS, reporting I4C].

**Stage 3: Isolation**
- Victims are told to stay on camera and tell no one, not even a spouse.
- Duration ranges from hours (Kochi) to 4 days (Rajkot), 13 days (Jabalpur), 40 days (Mumbai, ₹58 cr) and **6+ months (Bengaluru, ₹31.83 cr, watched over Skype in shifts)**.

**Stage 4: Moving the money**
- Pretext: "RBI verification", "a supervision account", "refund after the inquiry".
- Sources of money: **breaking FDs, gold loans (Pune: 30 tolas), selling investments**.
- **Mostly RTGS at the branch.** The Mumbai couple made 26 branch visits in 40 days; the Bengaluru victim made 187 transfers.
- Victims receive fake "clearance certificates", followed by more demands.

**Stage 5: Laundering and exit**
- **Mule accounts:** about 6,500 for one ₹58 cr case; holders are paid ₹2,500–5,000 per account.
- Money is converted to USDT and sent abroad.
- Operations run from compounds in Cambodia, Myanmar and Laos. I4C said 46% of Jan–Apr 2024 frauds came from there (≈₹1,776 cr).

## 2. The numbers

**Digital-arrest complaints and losses** (MHA reply to Parliament, via Inc42)

| Year | Complaints | Losses |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 39,925 | ₹91 cr |
| 2023 | 60,676 | ₹339 cr |
| 2024 | 1,23,672 | **₹1,935.5 cr** |

- **Complaints are now falling:** 58,249 in 2025 (down 52%) and 16,377 by 30 Jun 2026 (I4C to the Supreme Court). Losses per case stay high. Cumulative losses from 2022 to May 2026: ≈₹4,058 cr.
- **All cyber fraud:** ₹22,846 cr lost in 2024 [MHA]; ≈28 lakh cases and ₹22,931 cr in 2025 (cited in the RBI paper).
- **Supreme Court figures:** ≈₹3,000 cr extorted through digital arrests (Nov 2025); ≈₹54,000 cr siphoned by digital fraud overall (Feb 2026).
- **Largest losses:** ₹58.13 cr (Mumbai couple), ₹31.83 cr (Bengaluru), ₹23 cr (Delhi, 78-year-old ex-banker), ₹20.25 cr (Mumbai, 86-year-old).
- **Recovery is tiny:**
  - ">₹7,000 cr saved" through 1930/CFCFRMS by Dec 2025 [GOV], but "saved" means frozen.
  - **Only ₹18.05 cr was actually restored** to victims (Aug 2026) [COURT].
  - **Prevention matters far more than recovery.**
- **Golden hour:** report within 1–4 hours. A Mumbai sample of complaints made within 24 hours saw ≈91% recovered.
- **Gaps:** no national breakdown by age, and no official full-year 2025 digital-arrest loss figure.

## 3. What changed in 2025–26

**New scripts and AI**
- Deepfake judges and fake "virtual Supreme Court hearings", voice clones, "court-ordered house arrest", and translators for regional languages.

**Supreme Court** (suo motu case since Oct 2025)
- **1 Dec 2025:** gave the CBI a pan-India probe, including the role of bank staff.
- **9 Feb 2026:** ordered a national SOP, asked the RBI and DoT to work on victim compensation, and suggested banks alert customers on sudden withdrawals of ₹50–70 lakh.
- **4 Aug 2026:** told the RBI to issue a debit-hold SOP within 4 weeks; e-Zero FIR is now live in 19 states.
- Also: an RBIH–I4C data-sharing MoU (May 2026), a proposal to make digital arrest a separate offence (Jul 2026), and DoT, MeitY and I4C examining **time limits on audio and video calls**.

**Telecom**
- International spoofed-call blocking has been live since Oct 2024; calls from abroad are now labelled "International Call".
- **CNAP** (showing the caller's KYC name) had a March 2026 target; the full rollout is unconfirmed.
- **SIM binding for WhatsApp and Telegram** has been ordered, with the deadline reportedly moved to 31 Dec 2026.
- The order to pre-install **Sanchar Saathi** was withdrawn in Dec 2025.

**RBI**
- MuleHunter.AI is used by 23 banks (Dec 2025).
- **Discussion paper, 9 Apr 2026 (proposals only):**
  - a 1-hour hold on person-to-person transfers above ₹10k
  - **trusted-person approval for customers aged 70+ on transfers of ₹50k or more**
  - a kill switch
  - Transfers above ₹10k are **45% of frauds by count but 98.5% of the money lost**.
- Source: [rbi.org.in](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?id=23810). **No final direction has been found yet.**

**Public messaging**
- In Mann Ki Baat (27 Oct 2024) the PM gave three steps on digital arrest, reported in English as **"Stop, Think, Take action"**. Ruko means "stop", so **the brand matches a message India has already heard.**

## 4. Real saves and failures

**Saved at the bank branch (the most common save)**
- **Kochi, South Indian Bank:**
  - Signs the manager noticed: the customer was sweaty and rushed, made form errors, was paying a new out-of-state payee, and was on a live call.
  - What the manager did: asked **"Do you trust this payee?"**, which got the admission. Then said plainly that **"digital arrest does not exist"**, showed news and RBI videos, had him call 1930 and e-locked the account.
  - Result: ₹4.5 lakh saved, but ₹78k already sent by UPI was lost.
- **Ahmedabad, Bank of Baroda:** a customer broke all her FDs (₹27 lakh). The manager counselled her and called the cyber cell, and the scammers went silent once police arrived. **This was the fifth branch rescue that month.**
- **Mangaluru:** the couple claimed the ₹84 lakh was "to buy land". The manager stayed suspicious and called police.
- **SBI Hyderabad:** caught on the customer's 3rd visit to break FDs.
- **SBI Mahabubnagar:** a customer on a live video call couldn't explain what the RTGS was for.

**Saved by family or police**
- Pune: an 81-year-old father noticed his son was frightened and withdrawn.
- Bareilly: a Class 8 son switched the phone to flight mode.
- Mumbai: a daughter came home mid-call and phoned a helpline.
- Bhopal: police asked the fake officers for ID, and they hung up.

**Failures**
- **Rajkot (Sep 2026):** the bank warned her, so she paid ₹58.5 lakh **through a different bank**. One warning is not enough.
- **Mumbai, ₹58 cr:** 26 branch visits raised no flags, and the children abroad were never told.
- **Bengaluru, ₹31.83 cr:** 187 transfers over 6 months, reported a year late because of shame and a family wedding.
- The NCDRC (national consumer commission) is asking banks why they approved transfers even after victims raised alerts.

**Coached cover stories** ("say it's for a property"): likely, but not documented in primary sources.

## 5. Where a product can step in

| Stage | Where to act | How well it survives coaching |
|---|---|---|
| First call | Telecom labels, caller ID | Low |
| Video call, threats | An "Is this real?" check with fixed facts | Medium |
| Isolation (hours to days) | **Family** | **High: secrecy is the scammer's weak point** |
| Raising money (breaking FDs, gold loans) | Bank core-system signals | **High: behaviour, not what the victim says** |
| At the branch | Staff, script, 1930, e-lock | Medium; **fails after a single warning** |
| Online transfer | 1-hour hold + trusted-person approval | **High if a third party has to approve** |
| After payment | 1930 within 1–4 hours | Recovery is tiny |

## Top 10 implications for Ruko

1. Build the voice check around the **phrases scammers actually use**: parcel, Aadhaar misuse, TRAI, money laundering, stay on camera, don't tell anyone, RBI verification, refund after verification.
2. Lead with **one fixed sentence**: "No police, CBI, ED, RBI or court ever arrests anyone or collects money on a video call." That line ended the Kochi scam.
3. **Being told to keep it secret is the strongest red flag**, so give the elder one-tap family alert without needing to explain.
4. Watch for **signs of isolation** (a very long call, no reply to family, an unusual branch visit). Every family save started with someone noticing.
5. **Score bank risk on behaviour, not stated reasons:** breaking all FDs, a gold loan followed by RTGS, a new out-of-state payee, an amount far above normal, a live call, repeat visits.
6. **Keep the hold across visits and channels** until a trusted person or 1930 clears it, because of Rajkot.
7. Model trusted-person approval on the **RBI April 2026 proposal** (age 70+, ₹50k or more, 1-hour delay).
8. Give the fraud desk a **"Call 1930 now / e-lock account"** button and the branch script ("Do you trust this payee?", "Digital arrest does not exist").
9. Design for **scams that run over days and many instalments**. Track money lost before the save as its own metric.
10. Base drills on **current scripts** (courier, TRAI, terror, a cloned-voice "son arrested" call, a forged court order) and use the **"Stop, Think, Take action"** message plus 1930 and cybercrime.gov.in.
