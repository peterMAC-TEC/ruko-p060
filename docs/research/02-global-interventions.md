# Research 2: What has worked against these scams around the world (as of 9 Oct 2026)

How to read this:
- Source tags: **[REG]** regulator or government · **[ACAD]** peer-reviewed · **[NEWS]** reputable press · **[LAW]** law firm or consultancy · **[VENDOR]** self-reported by a company.
- Not verified, because the search limit was reached: friction data for individual UK banks, an evaluation of Stop! Think Fraud, Brazil's Pix night limits, Apple and Samsung call screening, and AFCX recovery totals.

## 1. United Kingdom

- **Mandatory refunds for scam victims (since Oct 2024): the strongest causal evidence in this report.**
  - Independent evaluation by Frontier Economics for the PSR (Jul 2026):
    - in-scope scam losses fell **≈£73m a year (≈21%)**, with **≈35,000 fewer scams**;
    - the reimbursement rate rose from 54% to 65%, and 97% of in-scope claims are now refunded;
    - **no sign that refunds made consumers careless** [REG].
  - First year: £173m refunded (an 88% rate). **71% of victims didn't know the policy existed.**
  - **Lesson:** making the sending *and* receiving banks share the cost (50:50) changed bank behaviour more than any awareness campaign.
  - Even so, total UK losses to this kind of scam still rose 19% to £576m in 2025, driven by investment scams.
- **Power to delay payments (Oct 2024):** banks may hold a suspicious outgoing payment for **up to 4 business days**. They must tell the customer what is needed to release it, and compensate them if the delay hit a genuine payment [REG].
- **Confirmation of Payee** (checks the payee's name): 2bn+ checks. **It does not stop impersonation scams**, because the victim knowingly pays a correctly named mule account.
- **Monzo "call status":** shows in the app whether a Monzo employee is really on the line right now. ≈700 fraud reports a month come through it; no effect on losses published [VENDOR].
- **Starling "Safe Phrases":** a campaign only, with no outcome data.
- **Banking Protocol** (branch staff plus a rapid police response): **£258m of fraud prevented** and 1,202 arrests since 2016, including £55.5m in 2022 [REG/industry].
- **Coached victims:** the Financial Ombudsman has ruled that bank questioning "would probably not have uncovered" a scam when the victim had been scripted to lie. Nationwide's Scam Checker refund pledge is void if the customer withheld information.
- **Telecoms:** Ofcom's landline anti-spoofing rule led to up to 1m calls a day blocked at BT. **O2's "Daisy" AI granny** has no evidence of reducing losses and is mainly PR.

## 2. Australia

- Scam losses fell from a peak of **A$3.1bn (2022) to A$2.03bn (2024)**, then **rose again to A$2.18bn (2025)**. Without continued pressure, the gains plateau [REG].
- **Scam-Safe Accord:** banks invested ≈A$100m in name checks and biometric checks on new accounts.
- **Scams Prevention Framework Act 2025:** obligations on banks, telcos and digital platforms from Mar 2027, with fines of up to ≈A$50m.
- **CommBank:** scam losses halved in FY24.
  - **NameCheck** stopped ≈A$40m of scam payments.
  - **CallerCheck** (confirms the bank is really calling) was used 3.8m times [VENDOR].
- **Westpac SaferPay:** asks contextual questions during the payment flow. 200k transfers were challenged and **A$194m abandoned** (not all of it scams) [VENDOR].
- **Apate.ai** (AI bots that talk to scammers, deployed with CommBank): no loss metrics published.
- **AFCX intelligence loop:** in a proof of concept, 62 reported scam texts led to 470k+ follow-on texts being blocked.

## 3. Singapore

- 2025 losses fell 19%, **but government-official impersonation more than doubled (losses up 60%)** as scammers **moved to in-person cash and gold handovers**.
  - **Lesson:** cover cash withdrawals too, not just digital transfers.
- **Money Lock** (locks savings so they can't be moved digitally):
  - **78,000 accounts and S$6.6bn locked by Mar 2024**, 44% of users aged 50+;
  - **no reported fraud on locked funds** [REG];
  - Hong Kong's equivalent had poor uptake (≈3,200 users). **Making it the default matters.**
- **Shared Responsibility Framework:**
  - a **12-hour cooling-off** after a new device;
  - a 24/7 **kill switch**;
  - a **rapid-draining rule**: if more than 50% of a balance of S$50k+ leaves within 24 hours, the bank must block or hold it;
  - liability falls on whichever party broke its duties (a "waterfall").
- **Protection from Scams Act (Jul 2025):** police can freeze the accounts of a victim who refuses to accept they are being scammed. Only 12 orders so far, and living-expense withdrawals were allowed.
- **ScamShield app:** 123k "unsure" checks resolved in Q1 2025.

## 4. Other countries

- **Brazil (Pix MED):** only **8.9% of money stolen in Pix scams has been returned**. Recovering money afterwards fails, so prevention is what matters.
- **EU Verification of Payee:** 14% of early checks came back "no match", which creates noise and warning fatigue.
- **Japan, a cautionary case:**
  - special-fraud losses hit a record **¥142bn in 2025**; seniors account for 59%, and fake-police scams doubled;
  - 280+ banks lowered ATM limits for elders, and Osaka capped ATM transfers for people 70+;
  - **AI cameras at ATMs detect someone on a phone call during a transfer and cancel it** (Tottori Shinkin, 2025);
  - **age limits displace fraud to other channels** rather than stopping it.
- **Hong Kong:**
  - **police use mule-account signals to phone likely victims proactively**: 4,060 cases and **HK$480m intercepted in 2025**, about **half of that year's drop in losses** [REG];
  - Scameter+ checks numbers and websites, and banks alert customers before a transfer to a flagged account.
- **Taiwan:** NT$12.9bn intercepted **at bank counters** (Jun 2023–Nov 2024).
- **USA:**
  - **FINRA trusted-contact and temporary-hold rules:** holds of up to 15 business days (extendable to 55), and the trusted contact must be told **within 2 business days** [REG];
  - FTC data: losses by people 60+ who each lost more than $100k rose 8x, from $55m (2020) to $445m (2024);
  - **Carefull and EverSafe** have published no prevention data.

## 5. Big-platform features

- **Google in-call protection:** if someone opens a banking app while screen-sharing on a call from an unknown number, Android shows a "Probable Scam" alert, a **one-tap hang-up and stop-sharing**, and a **forced 30-second pause**. Pilots run in the UK, India and Brazil, and in the US since Dec 2025. Google gives no numbers beyond "thousands" helped.
- **Truecaller Family Protection:** the admin can end flagged calls remotely (Android).
- **WhatsApp** (Oct 2025): a warning plus a forced "Continue" before screen-sharing with a non-contact on a video call.

## 6. Cross-cutting lessons

**Strong evidence:**
1. **Aligning liability plus the power to delay payments** (UK, −21%).
2. **The fraud desk proactively calling likely victims** (Hong Kong, half of the loss decline).
3. **Branch staff plus a rapid police response** (UK, £258m).
4. **Repeated, multi-touch education:**
   - a US Postal Inspection Service RCT cut revictimisation 22.4% with repeated mailings versus 8.6% with a single letter [ACAD];
   - inoculation improves detection without reducing trust in genuine messages, but **the effect decays and is specific to the channel trained**.

**Moderate evidence:**
- payee-name checks;
- contextual questions during the payment;
- **specific warnings beat generic ones**;
- letting customers check whether the bank is really calling (Monzo, CommBank).

**Weak evidence:** safe phrases, AI decoy bots, devices that warn when a call is recorded, and family monitoring apps.

**What failed:**
- warning fatigue (in a Japanese study, 35% of victims skipped platform warnings entirely);
- forewarning doesn't hold when the person is emotionally caught up;
- **coached victims lying to bank staff**;
- **scams displaced to cash handovers or other channels**;
- recovering money after payment;
- noisy name checks;
- opt-in protections that few people switch on.

**Design details that made the difference:**
- a **forced pause with a countdown**;
- **one-tap hang-up**;
- **unlocking that needs an in-person or separate-channel step**;
- **notifying the trusted contact within a fixed time limit**;
- a **clear path to release** a hold, with compensation if it was wrong;
- allowing living-expense withdrawals during a freeze;
- **signals the victim can't fake**: on a call or screen-sharing, a new payee, a new device, money draining fast.

## Top 12 ideas for Ruko

| # | Idea | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The fraud desk calls the victim proactively** when mule-account signals appear (Hong Kong) | Strong |
| 2 | **A cooling-off hold (hours up to 4 days) with a clear release path and a human callback** (UK, Singapore) | Strong (as a package) |
| 3 | **Notify the trusted contact automatically within a fixed time** whenever a hold fires (FINRA) | Moderate |
| 4 | **"Ruko Lock" on savings**: unlocking needs family or a branch visit; switched on by default at onboarding (Singapore Money Lock) | Moderate |
| 5 | **"On a call or screen-sharing while paying" as the top risk signal**, with a forced pause (Android, WhatsApp, Japan's ATM cameras) | Moderate |
| 6 | **Specific, contextual questions** instead of generic warnings (Westpac) | Moderate |
| 7 | **Warn people in advance about coaching** ("Scammers will tell you to lie to the bank"), and don't rely on what the victim says | Moderate |
| 8 | **"Is this call real?" backed by the bank's real list of who is calling**, plus the rule that no agency arrests anyone by video (Monzo, CommBank) | Moderate |
| 9 | **Check the payee's name and mule status before transfer**, tuned to avoid "no match" noise | Moderate |
| 10 | **Repeated drills**, not one-off training; measure how the effect decays | Strong (RCT) |
| 11 | **Cover cash and gold handovers**: flag large ATM or branch withdrawals and alert family (Singapore's displacement) | Moderate |
| 12 | **Pitch shared liability as Ruko's policy ask**, and report a false-alarm rate from harmless control calls | Strong (policy) / Moderate |

**Treat as nice-to-haves, not pillars:** safe phrases, AI decoy bots and call-recording warning devices. None has loss data, and Inagi city in Japan dropped its device programme.
